## Summary

Adopt ADR-0013 in the `orchestrator` skill: replace Step 3s's **global** leaf barrier ("wait for every in-flight leaf subagent, across all lanes") with a **per-lane** barrier, so a lane's inner join begins as soon as its own sub-lanes are DONE, concurrently with leaves still running elsewhere. In the same change, reprice the inner-join level in `references/config.md` from serialized `k × J` to concurrent slowest-of-`k` = `J`, exactly mirroring how the Step 2s architect level is already priced. The barrier and the charge move together — changing one without the other recreates the gate-and-ladder-on-two-accounts defect that *The cost side* and its worked example exist to prevent.

Adopting the ADR requires resolving the four open questions its *What must be resolved before implementing* section lists, plus three further hazards found in the skill text that the ADR does not name. This spec resolves all seven from the actual skill text, names every site that must change so the two halves stay in sync, states which worked-example figures move (none — all three are `k = 1`) and which new one must be added to lock the `k > 1` arithmetic, and handles the generated Prime Agent distribution.

## Goals

- Step 3s's barrier becomes per-lane: a lane's inner join starts when that lane's own sub-lanes are DONE, not when the whole leaf set is.
- The inner-join level is charged once, on the first adoption, at `J` task-equivalents — in `M_nested`, on the cost side, and on Step 2p.2's printed candidate line — so the gate, the printed candidate arithmetic, and the `ask` ladder's option-3 figure stay on one account.
- Every consequence of concurrency that the containment proof does *not* cover — deferred gates, parent-contract status writes, reconciliation determinism, `PARTIAL` routing, the contract-amendment loop, and the `max_parallel_lanes` in-flight ceiling — has an explicit, stated rule in the skill text.
- Two runs over the same contract still produce the same artifacts and the same printed `SUBJOIN` sequence.
- ADR-0013 moves `Proposed → Accepted` with its four questions answered in the record, and ADR-0012 / ADR-0014's "Not addressed here" cross-references are updated to match.
- `prime-agent/skills/**` is regenerated by the builder (never hand-edited) and `--check` plus `prime-agent/tests/parity.sh` pass.

## Non-goals

- **No new inner-join cost floor.** Under the new charge a second or third adoption whose sub-contract freezes no interface rows costs `c = 0`. That is the arithmetic ADR-0013 mandates and it is what `M_nested` actually carries; inventing a floor charge would put the gate and the ladder back on two accounts. See *Functional requirements* → FR-9 for the guards that remain.
- **No change to the Step 2s → Step 2L → Step 3L architect barrier.** `SKILL.md` → Step 2L's *"The honest cost, stated rather than glossed"* paragraph is about a **different** barrier (an unsplit lane's architect waiting on sub-contracts it will never read). Its follow-up is still a follow-up. Only its wording is disambiguated (FR-13), never its rule.
- **No change to `M_flat`.** ADR-0014 already filed the flat model's missing top-level integration lane as a separate item; this change does not touch it.
- **No change to `A`, `J`, or the `0.25` interface-point conversion, or to any default value.**
- **No change to the outer join's own barrier.** Step 3j still waits for every in-flight leaf; it gains one additional wait (FR-8), it does not lose the one it has.
- **No re-slice retry, no new config key, no new CLI arg.**
- **No commit and no push** — the pipeline ends at READY_TO_COMMIT.
- **No `.opencode` port.** `orchestrator` has no `.opencode/skills/orchestrator/` override (only `pr-review-report` and `spec-driven-eval` do), so the opencode-port-parity invariant does not apply here.

## Users and use cases

- **Orchestrator skill author (this repo's maintainer)** — adopts a `Proposed` ADR into the skill text with every open question answered from evidence, and leaves an audit trail a reviewer can re-derive. Success: a reviewer can check each of the seven resolutions against a quoted line of the skill, and can re-run all four worked examples by hand and get the stated figures.
- **The orchestrator skill itself, on a consumer project's `full` run with `k ≥ 2`** — pays one inner-join pass instead of `k`, so adoptions after the first no longer each have to clear a fresh `J`. Success: multi-lane `full` is reachable rather than theoretically available (ADR-0013 → *Expected effect*).
- **A user reading the `ask` ladder's option 3** — sees an `M_nested` figure that matches the cost the gate actually charged. Success: the gate verdict and the ladder figure agree on every worked example, including the new `k = 2` one.

## Functional requirements

### The barrier half

1. **Step 3s's global barrier is replaced by a per-lane barrier.** The sentence *"Wait for **every** in-flight leaf subagent — across all lanes, not just this lane's — to return before beginning any inner join"* (`SKILL.md`, Step 3s) is removed. In its place: a lane's inner join begins as soon as **that lane's own sub-lanes** have returned and passed the all-sub-lanes-DONE check, concurrently with leaves still running in other lanes. The existing per-lane check (*"Apply the all-leaves-DONE barrier here too, for this lane's sub-lanes"*, including its from-the-artifact verification, its single retry, and its `PARTIAL` routing) is retained verbatim in substance — it becomes the *only* barrier the inner join applies, rather than the second of two.

2. **The justification paragraph is rewritten, not deleted.** Step 3s's current paragraph ending *"…deliberately **not** taken at this depth. That is a spec-level change to the barrier discipline, recorded as a follow-up rather than assumed here"* describes the change as untaken. It must now state that the change **is** taken, name ADR-0013, and carry the containment proof forward as the operative justification: sub-lane globs strictly partition their parent lane's globs and lane globs are mutually disjoint, so no other lane's leaves can touch the paths a given inner join reconciles, and a lane's inner-join inputs are whole the moment its own sub-lanes return. The preceding containment paragraph (*"What the barrier buys is … not input wholeness"*) is preserved; it is the proof this change relies on.

3. **The pipeline-overview prose is corrected.** `SKILL.md` line ~105, *"**The joins compose bottom-up:** leaf barrier → every inner join (3s) → the outer join (3j)"*, asserts a global leaf barrier ahead of the inner joins and is false after FR-1. It must read as: per-lane sub-lane barrier → that lane's inner join (3s), overlapping other lanes' still-running leaves → the all-leaves barrier and the all-inner-joins barrier → the outer join (3j). The `simplify`/full-suite cadence sentence attached to it is unchanged. The ASCII diagram above it asserts no barrier and needs no structural change; the **3s** bullet at line ~102 gains the "begins as soon as that lane's own sub-lanes are DONE, concurrently with other lanes' leaves" clause.

### The seven concurrency resolutions

4. **Deferred-gate de-duplication (ADR-0013 question 1) — Step 3s no longer runs deferred gates; they move to the outer join.** Step 3s item 3's gate-running step is removed and the outer join's existing Step 3j item 4 becomes the single place any sub-lane-deferred gate runs. The rule the skill states must be: a sub-lane coder that meets a gate with no path-scoped form defers it, the inner join **collects and records** the deferral rather than running it, and every such gate is run once — de-duplicated, over the union — at Step 3j item 4, which already accepts *"any an inner join could not run at its own level"* and already blocks on a non-zero exit.

   The justification stated in the text must be the scope argument, not a locking argument: a deferred gate is **by definition** one with no path-scoped form, so it reads paths outside the lane and containment proves nothing about it. Step 3s item 3's premise — *"run each once over the **settled** lane union"* — is falsified by overlap, because other lanes' leaves are still writing. ADR-0013's option B (serialize just the gate-running sub-step behind a lock) is **rejected**: a lock makes the invocations sequential but does not settle the workspace the unscopable gate reads, so it fixes the concurrency of the command without fixing the correctness of its input. It also does not address the ADR's own warning that these are project commands which may not be concurrency-safe even over disjoint paths (shared build dirs, lockfiles, ports) — the outer join is the only point at which nothing else is in flight.

   Two consequences must be stated in the text: (a) Step 3s's `SUBJOIN` reconciliation of a lane no longer implies that lane's deferred gates passed — the deferral is *noted as such*, reusing the existing onward-deferral wording (*"A gate that has no path-scoped form at this level either defers once more, to the outer join, and is noted as such"*); (b) failure attribution must not be lost, so Step 3j item 4's failure output names the **lane(s) that deferred** the failing gate. The narrower-scope benefit Step 3s item 3 claimed (*"the smallest scope on which an unscopable gate is meaningful"*) is knowingly traded for correctness, and the trade must be stated rather than dropped.

   A third option — run the gate at 3s only when no other lane's leaves happen to be in flight — is **rejected on determinism**: it would place the same gate at different points in different runs.

5. **Parent-contract lane-status writes (ADR-0013 question 2) — confirmed safe, no change required beyond an explicit note.** `SKILL.md` → 3s.1 states *"The orchestrator writes the parent contract's lane-status table and every sub-contract's sub-lane-status table. **No subagent ever writes a `PACT` or a sub-contract**"*, and Step 3j item 5 repeats it (*"The **orchestrator is its sole writer** at both levels (Step 3s.1)"*). What overlaps under FR-1 is **subagent execution**, not the orchestrator's own steps: the orchestrator is one agent, so every Step 3s item-4 and item-5 table write is issued by that single writer and is therefore ordered by construction. This is the same property `SKILL.md` line ~594 relies on — *"Concurrency is a preference, not a correctness requirement … the joins (3s, 3j) are barriers on leaf completion, not on leaf simultaneity"*. 3s.1 must gain one sentence making the claim explicit under overlap: overlapping inner joins add no second writer, because the orchestrator performs each inner join's writes itself; only the leaf and integration coders it spawns run concurrently.

6. **Reconciliation determinism (ADR-0013 question 3) — determinism is re-established at the two observable surfaces.** Step 3s currently promises *"in a deterministic order (lane-map row order — never analysis order or completion order, so two runs over the same contract reconcile identically)"*. After FR-1 the **execution** order is wall-clock. The text must therefore separate execution order from observable order and pin the latter:

   - **Artifact writes.** Each sub-contract's sub-lane-status table is written by exactly one lane's inner join, so order is irrelevant to its content. The parent contract's lane-status table has one pre-existing row per lane; an inner join sets that lane's status cell only, so the settled table's content is identical regardless of the order the cells were set. The text must state this rather than leave it inferred — it is the whole reason artifact determinism survives.
   - **Printed output.** `SUBJOIN` blocks are **emitted in lane-map row order**, not completion order. The orchestrator holds each completed inner join's result and prints the full set in row order at a single point once every inner join has completed (immediately before Step 3j). Printing is not on the critical path, so ordering the emission costs nothing the overlap was bought for.
   - **Routing order.** Any `PARTIAL` / amendment routing arising from the inner joins is evaluated in **lane-map row order** (FR-7, FR-8), so that a run which exhausts `max_contract_amendments` consumes it in the same order every time.

   Net effect the text must assert: two runs over the same contract produce the same artifacts and the same printed `SUBJOIN` sequence; only wall-clock completion order differs.

7. **`PARTIAL` routing (ADR-0013 question 4) — recorded at the inner join, taken at the outer join.** An inner join that routes its lane to `PARTIAL` (a non-DONE sub-lane after the retry, or a `BLOCKED` sub-lane with a non-amendable reason) **records** the routing and marks the lane accordingly; it does not halt the run at that moment. Step 3j's *"Never abandon a running leaf — the leaves share one workspace, so abandoning one leaves that workspace in an unknown state"* is unchanged and is exactly why: a mid-run halt while other leaves are writing leaves the workspace in an unknown state. The `PARTIAL` halt (3j.1) is taken at Step 3j, after both barriers of FR-8 hold, at which point 3j's classification sees a fully settled picture — every leaf returned, every inner join complete. The text must state that this is what keeps 3j's classification consistent under overlap.

8. **Step 3j gains a second barrier: every inner join must have completed.** Step 3j currently asserts *"On a `full` run, **every inner join (Step 3s) has already completed** by the time this step begins"* — true only because of the global barrier FR-1 removes. After FR-1, an inner join (including its sequentially-invoked integration sub-lane coder, which writes files) can still be running when the last leaf returns. Step 3j's opening wait must therefore be **wait for every in-flight leaf subagent to return, and for every inner join to complete**, before anything else in the step. This is the barrier that makes FR-6's ordered emission and FR-7's deferred halt sound, and it is a requirement ADR-0013 does not name.

9. **Contract amendments crossing lane boundaries are deferred to the outer join.** `SKILL.md` → Step 3s states *"A `BLOCKED` sub-lane routes through the **same precedence rule** the outer join applies (3j.1 / 3j.2)"* but does not say **when** the loop is entered. Under overlap this matters, because 3j.2 step 5 invalidates leaves and step 7 re-dispatches them — and an **escalated parent-contract** amendment *"invalidates every sub-contract whose lane the amendment touches"*, which under overlap can include lanes whose leaves are still in flight. Invalidating an in-flight leaf contradicts *"Never abandon a running leaf"*.

   The rule the text must state: an inner join **records** the amendment routing and the amendment loop is entered at Step 3j, after both barriers of FR-8, with the requests evaluated in lane-map row order (FR-6). The **scoping** rule is unchanged — a sub-contract row still amends that sub-contract alone, an inherited parent row still escalates — so narrow amendment keeps its full blast-radius benefit; only the entry point moves. `max_contract_amendments` remains one shared budget with one writer (FR-5), consumed in a deterministic order (FR-6).

10. **The `max_parallel_lanes` ceiling counts inner-join integration coders.** `references/config.md` → `max_parallel_lanes` caps *"the number of architects Step 2L or coders Step 3L may have in flight at once"*, enforced by dispatching in waves. Under the global barrier an inner join's integration sub-lane coder ran alone; under overlap it runs concurrently with a leaf wave, so total in-flight coder width can exceed the ceiling. The key's two stated reasons — host concurrency and blast radius — both apply to that coder. The rule must therefore read: **at most `max_parallel_lanes` coder subagents are in flight at any time, counting each inner join's integration sub-lane coder**, i.e. Step 3L's wave sizing subtracts the number of inner-join integration coders currently running. This tightens nothing that was previously allowed (the old regime never had both in flight at once) and preserves the key's stated purpose. Both enforcement sites' wording (`SKILL.md` Steps 2L and 3L, and the `max_parallel_lanes` key) must remain mutually consistent; Step 2L is unaffected, since architect fan-out never overlaps an inner join.

### The charge half — change it in the same commit as the barrier, or neither

11. **`references/config.md` → *The makespan model*.** Three edits:

    - The bullet *"**Inner-join passes are serialized after the leaf barrier.** Step 3s reconciles each sub-split lane in turn, in lane-map row order, and every one of them sits on the critical path — so `k` of them cost the **sum of k**, i.e. `k × J`"* becomes the concurrent form: Step 3s begins a lane's inner join as soon as that lane's own sub-lanes are DONE, concurrently with leaves still running elsewhere, so `k` of them cost **slowest-of-`k`** — which, under the equal-effort assumption, is `J`, not `k × J`. It must cite `SKILL.md` → Step 3s and ADR-0013 the way the neighbouring bullets cite ADR-0012.
    - The `M_nested` block's line `+ k × J    k inner joins            (Step 3s — serialized after the leaf barrier)` becomes `+ J        k inner joins            (Step 3s — per-lane barrier, so slowest-of-k)`.
    - The framing sentence *"**The overhead term is split by shape, because the two levels have opposite shapes**"* is no longer true — after this change both levels are concurrent and cost slowest-of-`k`. It must be restated so the two bullets still say *why* each level is slowest-of-`k` (they are not interchangeable: 2s is concurrent because its architects are spawned together, 3s is concurrent because each lane's join is gated only on its own sub-lanes) without claiming an opposition that no longer exists.
    - The paragraph *"Charging the two levels as one undifferentiated overhead makes `M_nested` optimistic by roughly `(k − 1) × J`"* describes a superseded model. It is **preserved as recorded history**, not deleted — the file's established convention is to keep the defect it was corrected from (see the `min(leaf)` comparand note, the integration-slice note, and *Worked example — the gate verdict and the ladder figure must agree*) — with an explicit note that ADR-0013 supersedes the serialized reading by changing the machine, and that the ADR-0012-era `k × J` was correct for the barrier discipline in force at the time.

12. **`references/config.md` → *The cost side*.** The first bullet — *"one **inner-join pass** (Step 3s) — `J` task-equivalents, charged on **every** adoption. Because inner joins are **serialized**, a `k`-way nested plan carries `k × J` in aggregate, never `J`…"* — is rewritten to the same shape the sub-contract-architect bullet already has: `J` task-equivalents, charged on the **first** adoption only, because Step 3s's per-lane barrier lets the `k` inner joins overlap, so that whole level costs slowest-of-`k` = `J` no matter how many lanes are adopted. The first adopted candidate is what brings the inner-join level into existence, so it carries the charge; every later one rides it at no extra cost. The sentence must explicitly note that charging `J` per candidate would bill `k × J` for a level `M_nested` now prices at `J` — the identical failure the `A` bullet already names.

    The sequential-baseline paragraphs below it are **unchanged in value**: the first adoption on a sequential baseline still costs `A + A + J + J + I`, which is still exactly `M_nested`'s whole overhead term for `k = 1`. Every adoption after the first is now charged **that sub-contract's interface points only**.

13. **`SKILL.md` → Step 2p.2's `c` table and its trailing rule.** In the marginal-cost table, the "Every later adoption" cell for **both** baselines becomes `I(…)` alone, with the reason widened from *"the Step 2s level exists already and its architects run concurrently, so no second `A`"* to cover both levels: the Step 2s and Step 3s levels both already exist and both fan out concurrently, so no second `A` and no second `J`. The "First adoption" cells are unchanged (`A + J + I` on a flat baseline; `A + A + J + J + I` on a sequential one).

    The trailing sentence *"…never print a `k×J` aggregate on a candidate line — a candidate is charged **one** inner join, its own. `k × J` is a property of the assembled plan and belongs to `M_nested`"* is false in both halves after this change and must be replaced by the new rule: the inner-join level is charged once, on the first adoption, and a later candidate's printed `c` carries no `J` term at all. No `k × J` may remain anywhere in either file.

    The requirement that `g`, `c`, and `span` are printed term by term with values substituted is unchanged, and a later adoption whose sub-contract froze no rows prints `c = I(0×0.25=0) = 0` rather than a bare `0` — the expanded form is what makes a zero cost auditable rather than suspicious.

14. **Step 2L's "honest cost" paragraph is disambiguated, not changed.** `SKILL.md` → Step 2L's *"relaxing the sequence is a spec-level change to the barrier discipline, recorded as a follow-up and not taken here"* refers to the **2s → 2L architect** sequence, not to inner joins, and remains an untaken follow-up. Because ADR-0013 makes "the barrier discipline" ambiguous, that sentence must name which barrier it means so a later reader does not read ADR-0013's adoption as having taken it. Its arithmetic claim — `full` pays three serial architect round-trips where `lanes` pays two, charged as the Step 2s `A` — is untouched.

### Worked examples

15. **All three existing worked examples are re-derived and their figures re-stated; none of them move.** Each is `k = 1`, so `k × J` and `J` are the same quantity and every printed figure is unchanged. The plan must re-derive and confirm, not assume:

    | Worked example | `span_max` | overhead | makespan | `g` | `c` | verdict |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | *…the gate verdict and the ladder figure must agree* (`{12,6}` → `{6,6}`) | 6 | `A + A + J + J` = **8** | `M_nested` = **14** vs `M_flat` = **16** | **6** | **4** | adopted — unchanged |
    | *…one lane carries all the work* (`mobile` 24 → `{8,8,8}`) | 8 | `A + A + J + J + I(2×0.25)` = **8.5** | `M_nested` = **16.5** vs `M_seq` = **24** | **16** | **8.5** | adopted — unchanged |
    | *…a split carrying an integration sub-lane* (24 → 5 concurrent max + integration 6) | 11 | `A + A + J + J + I(8×0.25)` = **10** | `M_nested` = **21** vs `M_seq` = **24** | **13** | **10** | adopted — unchanged |

    Only the notation changes: the `1 × J` term in each overhead row is written `J`. Both existing examples carry a standing instruction — *"Re-check this example whenever either side of the model is edited"* — so the re-derivation is mandatory and its "unchanged" result must be stated explicitly, not left silent.

16. **A fourth worked example is added, exercising `k = 2` — the case the whole change exists for and the case no existing example covers.** The file's convention is one regression example per correction; without a `k > 1` example nothing in the repo pins the new arithmetic. The example, with its figures fixed here so the plan can assert them:

    Lanes `{A: 24, B: 10, C: 4}`; `T = 38`. Flat verdict **viable** (3 lanes carry work; largest holds `24/38` = 63% ≤ 70%), so the baseline is `M_flat`. Parent contract **0** interface points; each adopted sub-contract **2**. Defaults `A = 2`, `J = 2`.

    - Baseline: `span_base = max(24, 10, 4)` = **24**; `M_flat = 24 + A + J + I(0)` = **28**.
    - **Adoption 1** — split `A` into `{8, 8, 8}`, `integration: none`. `span(A)` = 8; new `span_max = max(8, 10, 4)` = **10**. `g = 24 − 10` = **14**; `c = A(2) + J(2) + I(2×0.25=0.5)` = **4.5**. `14 > 4.5` → **adopted**. Accumulated overhead `A + A + J + J + I(2×0.25)` = **8.5**; `M_nested` = **18.5**.
    - **Adoption 2** — recomputed critical lane is `B` = 10; split into `{5, 5}`, `integration: none`. `span(B)` = 5; new `span_max = max(8, 5, 4)` = **8**. `g = 10 − 8` = **2**; `c = I(2×0.25=0.5)` = **0.5** — no `A` (Step 2s level exists) and, newly, **no `J`** (Step 3s level exists). `2 > 0.5` → **adopted**. Accumulated overhead `A + A + J + J + I(4×0.25=1.0)` = **9**; `M_nested` = **17**.
    - **The behavior change this example pins.** Under the superseded `k × J` charge, adoption 2's cost was `J(2) + I(0.5)` = **2.5** against the same `g = 2`, so it was **rejected** — and that rejection was self-consistent then, because the serialized plan's own `M_nested` would have been `span_max(8) + A(2) + A(2) + 2 join passes(4) + J(2) + I(4×0.25=1.0)` = **19**, worse than the 18.5 of leaving `B` flat. The overlapped plan's `M_nested` is **17**, strictly better than 18.5. Gate and ladder agree on both sides of the change; that agreement is what the example checks.
    - **Gate/ladder reconciliation, stated in the example.** `M_nested` overhead (9) − `M_flat` overhead (4) = 5 = `c₁`(4.5) + `c₂`(0.5); and total span reduction 24 → 8 = 16 = `g₁`(14) + `g₂`(2). The remaining gates all pass: 6 leaves `{8,8,8,5,5,4}` sum to `T = 38`, largest holds 21% (≤ 70%), aggregate interface points 4 ≤ `T = 38`, and 6 leaves is within the `max_parallel_lanes` default of 6.
    - It carries the same standing instruction as the others: *re-check whenever either side of the model is edited*.

17. **The *Greedy, recomputed adoption* termination argument is corrected.** It currently reads *"The **per-adoption** increment to the overhead is `A + J` plus that sub-contract's interface points for the **first** adopted lane, and `J` plus interface points for every lane after it … Every adoption therefore lowers `span_max` while adding a strictly positive amount to the accumulated overhead"*. After FR-12 the later-adoption increment is **interface points alone**, which is **zero** when a sub-contract freezes no rows — so the "strictly positive" claim is false and must not be left standing. The corrected text must:

    - restate the increment: `A + J` plus interface points for the first adopted lane, and that sub-contract's interface points alone for every lane after it, because **both** the Step 2s and Step 3s levels are brought into existence once and paid for once;
    - restate termination on the terms that actually still hold — greedy critical-lane-first evaluation means each later adoption is measured against a strictly smaller remaining gain, `g > c` still rejects a wash (`0 > 0` is false, so a zero-gain candidate is rejected even at zero cost), and the lane set is finite;
    - name the three independent guards that bound a cheap later adoption: the `max_parallel_lanes` leaf-width ceiling, the aggregate diminishing-payback rule, and the per-sub-lane re-application of the viability conditions. This is the honest replacement for the "strictly positive cost" argument and must be stated as such rather than glossed.

## Decisions resolved by Brainstormer default

- **ADR-0013 question 1 — which gate-de-duplication option?** → **Option A, generalized: every sub-lane-deferred gate moves to the outer join; Step 3s stops running gates entirely.** → An unscopable gate reads outside the lane by definition, so containment proves nothing about it and overlap falsifies Step 3s item 3's "settled lane union" premise; option B's lock serializes the invocation without settling its input, and the outer join is the only point at which nothing else is in flight (which also answers the ADR's own shared-build-dir/lockfile/port warning). Coverage is unchanged — Step 3j item 4 already exists, de-duplicates, blocks, and accepts gates an inner join could not run. (FR-4)
- **A third gate option — run at 3s only when no other lane is in flight?** → **Rejected.** → It places the same gate at different points in different runs, breaking the determinism FR-6 has to preserve.
- **ADR-0013 question 2 — are parent-contract lane-status writes safe?** → **Yes, confirmed from 3s.1 and Step 3j item 5; add one explicit sentence rather than a mechanism.** → The orchestrator is one agent and is the sole writer; what overlaps is subagent execution, not orchestrator steps (`SKILL.md` ~L594: the joins are barriers on leaf *completion*, not *simultaneity*). No lock, no ordering protocol. (FR-5)
- **ADR-0013 question 3 — how is determinism re-established?** → **Execution order becomes wall-clock; observable order is pinned — artifacts are order-independent by content, `SUBJOIN` blocks are emitted in lane-map row order once all inner joins complete, routing decisions are evaluated in lane-map row order.** → Deferring the print costs nothing the overlap bought, since printing is not on the critical path; the alternative (print on completion, accept a nondeterministic transcript) trades an observable guarantee for nothing. (FR-6)
- **ADR-0013 question 4 — does `PARTIAL` routing stay consistent?** → **Yes, provided the halt is *recorded* at the inner join and *taken* at the outer join, after the FR-8 barriers.** → "Never abandon a running leaf" already forbids halting while leaves write; taking the halt at 3j is what lets 3j's classification see every leaf returned and every inner join complete. (FR-7)

(FR-8)
- **Hazard not in the ADR — the contract-amendment loop can invalidate an in-flight leaf.** → **Record amendment routing at the inner join; enter the loop at Step 3j in lane-map row order.
