The Unmeasured Gate

A 15-hour run spent every budget it had and shipped anyway. Its one gate for test quality reported UNMEASURED. The branch written to fix that gate makes it report a number instead — and the number is wrong in the direction that hides bugs.

The pipeline

Containment worked. Convergence didn't.

The P1–P8 fixes from the last forensics all shipped and all held: the cascade stayed inside one family, cycles were stamped honestly, the eval ran in-loop. Nothing escaped.

What replaced escape is saturation. Review 6/6, eval 2/2, family 6/6 — every ceiling reached inside one invocation, and no meter anywhere counts anything but cycles.

The mutation_test integration

Not ready to be the default.

Three defects reproduced against reports the real tool emits. The worst scores un-run mutants as kills, so G6 can return pass on code where most mutants survived.

The swap away from dart_mutant is correct and well-founded — that tool was silently unusable here. The replacement just isn't finished.

Part I · The run

What the last round of fixes actually bought

This is the third forensic pass on the same symptom. The first two — The Rework Cascade over lotery_oracle and toodls, and the run-cost audit over toodls' 75-hour run — produced eight ranked fixes. All eight are in the tree: requirement-coverage maps (4313210), the eval moved inside the review loop (c59432c), thresholds single-sourced from config (967a9e4), phase-exit gates (93989e9), the family rework budget (f830043), the parallel path demoted (59e1887), one protocol injection per session (de6ca06).

b3-app is the first full run after all of them, and it is the right test. The failure mode changed shape.

The leak is closed. The requirement map carried all 53 requirements to every reviewer pass, and the reviewer re-verified them against the run's root plan on each cycle exactly as designed. What the fixes did not touch is the thing that made six cycles necessary in the first place — and one of them, the eval moved in-loop, now spends from the same budget the reviewer just converged inside.

Where the 904 minutes went

Two numbers carry the whole diagnosis. The review loop ran 9h45m — 64.7% of the run — and moved lib/ by +226/−26 net against the build phase's +2538/−21. Under 9%. Three of the five remediation cycles changed zero files under lib/, and production code is byte-identical from 07:24:40 through HEAD.

The coupling hypothesis — fixes breaking each other — is dead on its own terms. All 17 suite rows in the ledger are pass or flake; not one RED exists in the run, and every flake is the same grpc-dart TLS teardown race the eval characterised as "a false RED under concurrency, never a false green." The run's genuine architecture findings (three copies of PinnedChannelCredentials, session-expiry implemented twice) were filed in the first review and never touched in six cycles.

What the loop was actually doing was raising assertion adequacy — finding, one or two per cycle, guards that could not fail. That work was real and it found fourteen live defects. The defect is the scheduling, not the method.

Ranked: what generated the cycles

Each claim below was put to an independent verifier instructed to default to refuted and to re-derive every number from source. One survived intact; nine were weakened. The materiality figures are the verifiers' corrected ones, not the finders'.

01

The eval re-opens a loop the reviewer has already closed, and its PASS bar is a perfect score

weakened

SKILL.md Step 4e · spec-driven-eval/SKILL.md · 215.8 min · 23.9% of the run · 2 of 6 review slots

At 09:28:34 the reviewer returned APPROVED with must_fix_count: 0. Three minutes later the spec eval returned ISSUES at 0.94, "Spec-complete", with 53 of 53 requirements verified and none unmet — and reopened the loop for two more full FIX→TEST→CR cycles that changed nothing under lib/ and moved the score to 0.98.

The mechanism is not "no severity floor" — it is that the gap list is defined as "the fixes needed to reach 1.00", so ISSUES derives from that list being non-empty. On any non-trivial spec, PASS is practically unreachable. Both cycles spent from the same budget the reviewer had just converged inside, and the two items the eval itself left open (FR1, FR5) now need --override-family-budget to close.

What cuts against it: that cost bought the run's only assertion that mTLS validates the CA. A naive score floor would have suppressed a real security gap. The floor has to be on kind, not on grade.

02

Nothing meters anything but cycles

confirmed

All nine bounds count cycles or lane width · no token, cost, or wall-clock meter exists

Review 10, QA 5, eval 2, family 6, amendments 2, lanes 6 — every budget in the harness meters rework or concurrency. None meters spend. A run cannot answer "how long have I been going" or "how much has this cost", so nothing can stop on either.

The one wall-clock bound the skill claims to own is dead code. BLOCKED_STALE is accepted as a qa_status at SKILL.md:1258, given a stop banner at :1282, routed to the timeline render at :1529, styled in render-artifact.cjs, and branched on by both product-manager and validation-fixer — while its sole producer, a stale_gates: frontmatter key, is written by nothing in the repository. templates/qa.md has no Step 0 and no timeout rule. That is exactly the bound that would have stopped the 43-minute dart_mutant hang.

Correction to the finder: timeouts do exist in the gate runner — dart-flutter.cjs:142 and :566, both 120 s. They bound tool-availability probes, never the gate body.

03

Spec size is admitted without a ceiling, and it is a real risk multiplier

weakened

53 requirements · 145 tasks · 122 files · 22,587 insertions, as one run

SKILL.md Step 2 counts the spec's numbered requirements at what it calls "the cheapest point in the whole run", uses the count for an equality check against the plan's coverage map, and throws the magnitude away. The requirement count is taken at three separate sites and ceilinged at none.

Rebuilding the corpus — 218 specs across the orchestrator projects on this machine — 53 requirements is the 96.6th percentile (median 14, p75 ≈ 25, p90 40, max 86). Size shifts review volume: mean CRs per family rises 2.0 → 3.2–3.5, Spearman ρ = 0.43, and the share of families needing 3+ review rounds climbs from 23% to 68–75% (p = 1.9e-4).

What cuts against it: the finder's headline "3% → 29% runaway rate" does not reproduce under any family definition tried — at the 4+ round mark the effect vanishes entirely (p = 0.41). The corpus's worst family is a 12-requirement spec that took 13 CRs; its largest spec (86 requirements) took 3. The corpus predicts ~3 CRs for a 53-requirement spec and this run took 6, so at most half this run's review cost is attributable to size. Size is a multiplier, not a threshold.

04

A first run on a fresh spec can legally spend the entire cross-run family budget

weakened

SKILL.md:296 · review_budget = min(10, max_family_cycles − 0) = 6

max_review_cycles: 10 is non-binding on every fresh spec, and --max-review can only lower the effective number, never raise it. That subordination is intended and it worked. The defect is that the binding number is invisible while it is being spent: printed once at Step 0b, once in the FINAL, persisted in no artifact — grep -rn review_budget over b3-app's plans/ returns zero hits — with no proximity warning anywhere.

The consequence is now live: all eight items in the FINAL's follow-up backlog must either override the family gate or evade it by minting a fresh spec, which is precisely the cascade that gate exists to stop.

05

No role is asked to generalise a finding to its class

weakened

templates/*.md · 0 hits for sweep / inventory / same-class / exhaustive · ~1 cycle, not five

The contract is instance-shaped throughout: architect.md:335 turns each Must Fix into exactly one task pair, coder.md:336 forbids work outside the plan's tasks, and architect.md:147 authorises narrowing but never widening. Cycle 1 filed the class as a class — four sites in one Should Fix — the FIX plan closed exactly those four, and cycle 2's own acceptance row records the result verbatim: "All four closed… The class of defect is not closed."

I overstated this earlier and the verifier is right to cut it. The pipeline generalised anyway, without being asked: a 39-row guard inventory existed by cycle 2 and was rebuilt to 38 call sites by cycle 4. The missing contract cost roughly one cycle, not five. The remaining cost belongs to bypass proofs that were inherited rather than executed — and to G6, the gate that would have caught exactly this, producing nothing.

The counterfactual is still worth having: cycle 3's exhaustive sweep — 38 call sites, 46 executed mutations, 10 guards repaired — took 59 minutes, and the next CR found zero new instances of the class.

06

The reviewer re-reads the whole tree every cycle, and this — not the protocol text — is the dominant read cost

weakened

Step 1.3 snapshot: 4,967,756 B over six cycles vs a ~1.97 MB protocol floor

The finder's headline was a 2.1 MB fixed scaffolding floor re-read by all 26 role invocations, 39% of it one 29 KB file. That floor is real (~1.97 MB recomputed), and 21.8% of it is measurably dead weight — the html-view and parallel-mode blocks of artifact-format.md, structurally inapplicable to this run's output_format: md sequential mode.

But it is only 13–25% of read volume. The reviewer's per-cycle whole-tree snapshot is 12× larger. MAESTRO_PREV_CR_REF is referenced in the reviewer template and set by nothing — the snapshot base never moves, so cycle 6 re-reads the same union cycle 1 did. The repo already knows: SKILL.md:502 and ADR-0018:73 both name it as the canonical example of "a line every role carries and none uses". Five of six CRs record "narrowed nothing"; reviewers narrowed anyway, on their own judgement, from cycle 4.

07

Suite inheritance is nearly unreachable — but pinning .gitignore would not have helped

causally refuted

17 ledger rows · 17 distinct (suite, tree) pairs · 1 inheritance opportunity, taken

The mechanism is real: flutter test re-appends .build/ and .swiftpm/ to the tracked .gitignore, which sits inside ADR-0018's tree-hash recipe, so a test run can move the boundary hash by itself. It did — once, at the final tester→QA handoff.

It is not why inheritance was rare. The .gitignore blob is constant across six consecutive recorded trees, covering cycles 1–5 and at least ten flutter test executions. Seven of eight tree transitions moved because 2–105 source files changed, where re-executing is correct. The 1-of-17 ratio is not a miss rate — the run offered exactly one inheritance opportunity and took it. Pinning those two lines saves approximately zero. Worth doing for hygiene; do not bill it as a cost fix.

Part II · The gate

mutation_test, measured against the tool it wraps

First, the premise: the swap is justified. dart_mutant 0.4.0 was not hanging — it was silently ignoring the flags the adapter depends on. --test-command is a dead option; the binary hardcodes dart test --reporter=compact. --glob is a no-op — an impossible glob still walks the whole tree — and because the adapter's --exclude list replaces the tool's defaults (which contain **/test/**), the workload inflated from 1,185 to 6,815 mutants.

On b3-app, dart test cannot run at all — the project has no direct test dev-dependency, only flutter_test — so every mutant fails in ~3 s with exit 65, which dart_mutant scores as Killed. Reproduced on a scratch copy: 986 mutants, 986 killed, 0 survived, mutation score 100.0.

The most important thing in this report about the old tool

If dart_mutant had finished, G6 would have reported a perfect score on a suite it never ran. UNMEASURED was the better outcome. The 43-minute non-convergence protected the run from a fabricated green. "0.4–1.4% CPU, zero child test processes" was an observation artefact — the parent is a supervisor, and its children are dart, never the flutter the QA agent grepped for.

Three defects, one reproduction

The adapter parses the junit report with a regex over <testsuite> attributes and <testcase> pairs. Reading mutation_test 1.8.0's own writer settles what those mean:

xunit_report.dart:98–105 and _addTestCases:132–168
OutcomeMarkup the tool emitsCounted in
Detected (killed)no child element — the testcase is self-closingtests
Undetected (survived)<failure type="undetected">tests, failures
Timeout<error type="timeout">tests, errors
Not covered<error type="not covered by tests">tests, errors

The adapter computes (tests − failures) / tests and never reads errors. Feeding it a document shaped exactly as the writer produces — one killed, one survived, one not-covered, one timed out:

$ node -e '…parseMutationTestJunit(realShapedReport)'

ADAPTER  score: 75.0%   total: 4   byFile: {"lib/strong.dart":[20]}
TRUTH    detected = 1 of 4 → 25.0% ;  the only survivor is lib/weak.dart:20

Both halves of that output are wrong, and both fail safe-side-down

The score is inflated. Timed-out and not-covered mutants are scored as kills. With the configured threshold of 70, this document passes — on code where three of four mutants survived. A mutant that was never executed is not a mutant the tests killed.

The file attribution is inverted. Killed mutants are self-closing, so the paired-tag regex takes the killed mutant's classname and swallows forward to the next real </testcase>, pairing it with a later survivor's failure body. The well-tested file gets blamed; the weak file gets a clean bill. On a three-file probe the attribution was measured 100% wrong and perfectly inverted — and this output is what steers a fixer agent.

Third: a scope yielding no mutants emits a 35-byte <testsuites/>, which fails the parser's /<testsuite\b/ guard, so the gate returns error and exit 4. 31 of b3-app's 95 lib files — 32.6% — yield zero mutants, so a third of the project is a false error today.

These three are one defect surface: the parser pairs text where it needs to walk elements. Nothing about the score arithmetic is salvageable from attributes alone, because errors= fuses timeout and not-covered and no attribute separates them.

Part III · What to do

The fix, in order

Ordered by impact over effort, with the gate before the harness — one of these ships a wrong number today, the rest cost time.

Before this branch merges

  1. Replace the junit regex with a tag walker. Node ships no XML parser and this .cjs takes no dependencies, but a 44-line tag-level tokeniser is enough: it makes self-closing and paired elements the same event, which is what lets "detected" be inferred from the absence of a child. Score becomes detected / total — the pessimistic bound, so a pass holds even if every timed-out mutant had survived — with measuredScore reported beside it. Guard on <testsuites, not <testsuite, so an empty scope passes instead of erroring. Cross-check Σtests against the elements counted and return null on mismatch, so a truncated report is an error rather than "zero mutants".
  2. Report the four outcomes as four rules — mutation/survived, mutation/not-covered, mutation/timeout, mutation/blocked. Survived and not-covered are both real debt but the fix differs: write a stronger assertion, versus write any test at all. Route an unrecognised type string to "unmeasured", never to "killed", so a future 1.9.0 degrades safely.
  3. Bound the run, then bound the tree. A timeout on the execFileSync with killSignal: 'SIGKILL' — ETIMEDOUT is cleanly distinguishable from mutation_test's own non-zero quality-gate exit, so the partial report gets discarded rather than scored. Then register SIGINT/SIGTERM/SIGHUP listeners: registering is the fix, not the handler body — a listener makes the signal non-fatal so the blocking call returns and finally restores. Residual after that is SIGKILL only, and the doc that suggests bare timeout 900 becomes wrong and must change to timeout -k 30.
  4. Add the preflight, and let the gate decline. mutation_test -d loads the same config and the same lcov, runs no tests, and returns the exact count the scoring run would produce — 0.52 s. Multiply chargeable mutants by the per-mutant cap and refuse the run when it exceeds the budget. b3-app's lib then gets declined in about a second with reason: 'mutant-cap', mutants: 826, worstCaseSeconds: 99120, instead of being discovered 43 minutes into a hang. Use the cap, not a mean — measured mean was 0.85 s/mutant against two mutants costing 30.8 s each.
  5. Make UNMEASURED a first-class state. A pass that measured nothing must not be byte-identical to a real pass. measurement.state ∈ {empty, measured, partial, unmeasured} beside status: a consumer asking "did it pass" reads status; one asking "was this actually verified" reads state. This is the repo's own stated invariant and the gate currently violates it.
  6. Add the fallback you asked for — unpinned resolves mutation_test, then dart_mutant, then missingTool; an explicit pin never silently switches tools; and g6Verdict is told which one ran so the report never attributes one tool's score to the other.
  7. Fix the parity gate — restore the skill-dir bullet and re-point the overlay, rather than deleting the overlay entry and stranding <skill-dir>, which SKILL.md uses twice and defines nowhere. Then correct the sandboxing contradiction, expected="0", the dart_mutant install hint, and reviewer.md's stale MISSING_TOOL claim in the same change.

And say this in the docs

G6 over --scope project on a suite of b3-app's size is not a per-change gate. What fits an 1800 s budget is --scope diff and narrow modules. A project-wide mutation score is a scheduled artifact.

For the pipeline

  1. Give the run a meter. max_run_minutes (default 0 = off), bound at Step 0b from boundaries[0].at, evaluated at every boundary the run already mints. Not to kill the run — to make it able to say what it has spent, which is the precondition for every other stop rule.
  2. Either wire BLOCKED_STALE or delete it. Wiring it means a real ## Step 0 — Gate wall-clock budget in qa.md that writes stale_gates:. Leaving vestigial machinery branched on by three downstream skills is the worse option.
  3. Reserve eval headroom and floor the loop on kind. review_budget = min(max_review_cycles, max_family_cycles − family_cr_count − max_eval_cycles), so eval remediation cannot strand its own findings. Then let Step 4e reopen only for a confirmed red, a requirement the root map marks Met-by-plan that the eval scores unmet, or an explicitly security-classed gap — everything else lands in the FINAL's issues list. The mTLS gap this run found would still pass that filter; the eleven "missing assertions over already-correct behaviour" would not.
  4. Make the binding budget visible. Stamp review_budget and family_cr_count into every CR's frontmatter, and warn at two remaining. The run only learned its budget was gone by reading its own final report.
  5. Add a scope band at Step 2, where the count is already taken: silent to p75 (~25 requirements), advisory to ~35 with the percentile printed, and above that a stop that prints a mechanical split derived from the spec's own sub-headings and the plan's phase boundaries — each of which already exits on a green tree. Calibrate against the corpus, and state honestly that size predicts about half the effect.
  6. Require a class census on repeat findings. When a CR files two or more findings sharing a root cause, the FIX plan's scope is every occurrence of that idiom in the tree, enumerated with a count — not the file:line the CR cited. Cheap, and worth about one cycle.
  7. Set MAESTRO_PREV_CR_REF from the previous 4c boundary tree, which the ledger already records. This is the 12× read cost, not the protocol text — though splitting the html-view and parallel-mode blocks out of artifact-format.md is a free 21.8% off the floor for md-format sequential runs.

Not worth doing: pinning .gitignore for inheritance. Real mechanism, ~zero saving. Do it as hygiene if you like, but it is not a cost fix.

Coda

What I could not settle

  • Whether the six cycles were avoidable at all. Fourteen real defects were found, and every one was caught by executing a mutation rather than by reading. If G6 had worked, most of them would have surfaced in one QA pass instead of six review cycles — but G6 has never produced a number on this stack, so that counterfactual is untested. It is also the strongest argument for finishing this branch properly.
  • The "at most 14 of 59 findings are regressions" figure does not reproduce. Under a loose definition it is 40 of 59; under a tight one, 1 of 45. The finder's number sits in neither place and had no stated operationalisation. The conclusion it supported survives on other evidence; the number should not be quoted.
  • Whether parallelism: "ask" ever evaluated this run. It was armed, 145 tasks across 10 phases were admitted, and no trace exists that the viability step ran. If it ran and declined, that is a data point about the split heuristics pointing at concurrency rather than at size.
  • Progress-log timestamps are model-authored. They cannot be used to attribute cost. Every timing in this report comes from ledger boundaries and artifact frontmatter instead.

Method — 22 agents across two workflows: 11 investigation dimensions over the run artifacts, the harness, and the adapter; then 10 adversarial verifiers instructed to default to refuted and to re-derive every cited number from source, plus one patch designer that generated real mutation_test 1.8.0 reports on a throwaway package and executed each fix against them. Nine of ten claims came back weakened; the corrections are in the text above rather than in a footnote. The three parser defects and the parity failure were reproduced by hand before publication. Nothing in b3-app or my-skills was modified — git status --porcelain in both is unchanged, and b3-app's HEAD is still b48998e.
